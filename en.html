In English

Danilo Bortoli

lawyer · regulatory intelligence · institutional accountability

I'm a lawyer in Brazil. I work in public law, regulatory intelligence, and the enforcement of institutional accountability.

Practice

Most of what I do involves making institutions answer for themselves. I litigate complex commercial and tax disputes. I serve as legal coordinator for MATRA (Marília Transparente), a civil society organization that monitors public procurement and challenges irregularities in local governance — filing complaints before courts of auditors and the Federal Prosecution Service, obtaining documents under freedom-of-information law, investigating contracts worth tens of millions of reais.

Alongside the legal work, I build. I've developed automated systems for monitoring official gazettes, flagging anomalies in procurement data, and integrating public databases that were never designed to talk to each other. I think about law as an information problem: the question isn't only what does the rule say, but how does the institution actually behave — and what tools, legal and technical, can bring those two things closer together.

Writing

I've written for PopMatters on Bernard Harcourt's Exposed and Cass Sunstein's Constitutional Personae. I contribute to The Singles Jukebox. I kept a year-long One Week One Band on Burial.

Open to

Roles and conversations in compliance, investigations, regulatory intelligence, or government affairs — especially where law meets technology and public accountability. I know the Brazilian regulatory environment well, and have no illusions about how Brazilian institutions actually work.

Fluent in English. Open to relocation.
