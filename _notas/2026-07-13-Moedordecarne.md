Matthew Gasda no diário de escritor dele [ontem](https://open.substack.com/pub/matthewgasda/p/writers-diary-436?r=4mtyz&utm_medium=ios):

> The collective technological crisis is growing clumsy and more obvious, and the very breakdown of productivity and communication, grace and decency, is actually an opening. But someone just has to name the way forward.

> Too much time and human energy is spent building an infernal machine, THE infernal machine: mimetic destruction, mimetic snares, and financial and moral and spiritual snares. And it all happens unthinkingly, in the up and down and sideways swiping and scrolling of the unaesthetic, sluggish world.

> We need prophets of this extremity—darker, more oracular, more enigmatic than even Heidegger and Nietzsche.

> It’s not that AI is the endpoint; ai is the skeleton key or the meta code which reveals how inchoate and undeveloped and sad we have been for a long time, unsure of how to find and energetically fund lasting bonds with ourselves, with nature, with our children, with our parents, with our neighbors, with strangers.

> In World War I, there was a Christmas truce after the first six months of fighting, so the soldiers could exchange gifts and play soccer, famously. Aside from the fact that the war began at all, the fact that it continued after this point is one of the great tragedies of Western history.

> If everyone stopped feeding themselves into the infernal machine, stopped performing mimetic sacrifices, stopped putting their friends and others and enemies on the conveyor belt towards the symbolic meat grinder, just simply stopped and sat up and shook hands.

> And maybe the very pervasiveness of the crisis could cease its mindless repetition and start to evoke its own cure.

> (The most striking thing about the period from 2012 till now is that we’re still here. Everyone stopped living in the world at once, and the world continued without us, and even supported us, like crash outs who return home to live in the basement.)
